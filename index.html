01About

I build machine-learning models that represent biological systems, learn their dynamics, and generate molecules where data are scarce — grounded in physics and mechanism.

I am passionate about self-supervised learning, cross-modal representation learning, generative modeling and geometric deep learning.

  1. 01

    Model ⟷, and its machine-learning conjugate: Representation

    Learn real-world structure.

    A scientific model is a hand-built representation of a system; its learned conjugate is discovered from data. I learn self-supervised, cross-modal embeddings from unlabeled views of the same sample, build on pretrained foundation models, and keep representations stable and informative.

    • Cross-modal JEPA →
    • HuggingLigand →
    • Notch surrogate →
  2. 02

    Simulation ⟷, and its machine-learning conjugate: Generation

    Create data where it is thin.

    Simulation runs a model forward; a generative model learns to do the same from examples. I use property-guided, E(3)-equivariant diffusion and geometry-driven scene synthesis to produce data in the sparse regions that measurement rarely reaches.

    • QALPA →
    • Surgical scenes →
    • Photoacoustics →
  3. 03

    Physics ⟷, and its machine-learning conjugate: Constraint & understanding

    Physics and mechanism in the loop.

    Physics is the check on the other two corners. Coupling learning with quantum chemistry, mechanistic ODEs and multiphysics simulation keeps models honest — checked against, and improved by, physical evaluation — and keeps what they learn interpretable.

    • QALPA →
    • Notch surrogate →
    • Photoacoustics →

Trained in biomedical engineering at Cairo University and computational life science at TU Dresden, I’ve worked across molecular generation, representation learning, bioimage analysis and simulation for surgical robotics.

02 Generation03 ConstraintCourse project · Preprint 2026

QALPA — Property-guided molecular generation

Interesting molecules sit where we have no data.

An active-learning loop: an E(3)-equivariant diffusion model proposes 3D molecules, MACE or SO3LR relax and filter them, DFT with FHI-aims evaluates the survivors, and each round the generator is steered by what the physics confirmed. An interactive interface lets users navigate the molecular property landscape.

  • Equivariant diffusion
  • Active learning
  • MACE · SO3LR
  • FHI-aims

Hybrid mechanistic–neural modeling of tissue morphogenesis

Mechanism and learning in one model.

Notch signaling is a well-understood ODE system, but too slow to solve in every cell of a tissue simulation. A BayesFlow neural surrogate replaces the per-cell solver in Morpheus — and the simplest network won.

Learning

02 Simulation03 PhysicsM.Sc. thesis · Cairo University

Multiphysics model of photoacoustic plaque detection

Can light-generated sound reveal what is inside tissue?

A coupled Monte Carlo–finite-element framework. Starting from segmented anatomy, mesh-based Monte Carlo gives the light fluence, bioheat transfer and solid mechanics turn absorbed light into motion, and pressure acoustics carries the signal to surface detectors — here, to reveal plaque in a vessel.

The question it left me with: can a cheaper, learned simulation give the same output?

  • Finite elements
